12 Errores Comunes en una Reforma (y Cómo Evitarlos sin Morir de Miedo)

Junio 2025 10 min lectura

Nadie empieza una reforma queriendo cometer errores. Pero la realidad es que la mayoría de las personas que reforman su vivienda en Bilbao cometen los mismos fallos — porque nadie les avisa de qué deben vigilar.

Después de asesorar a cientos de propietarios en Bilbao y Bizkaia durante sus reformas, hemos identificado los 12 errores que más dinero cuesta, más estrés generan y más fáciles son de evitar.

The 12 Mistakes That Derail Renovations

  1. Only requesting one budget — This is the mother of all mistakes. In Bilbao, the price difference between the lowest and highest budget for the same work can be 30-40%. Always request at least 3 detailed budgets from different companies. And "detailed" is key: a budget that just says "bathroom reform — 8,000€" is useless. You need line-by-line breakdown.
  2. Not setting a contingency budget — In older Bilbao apartments (Santutxu, Casco Viejo, Indautxu), where most renovations happen, there's almost always hidden problems: lead pipes, old electrical wiring without grounding, rising damp in ground floors. Always add 15-20% extra on top of your budget. In buildings over 40 years old, make it 20-25%.
  3. Choosing the cheapest budget — If a budget for a full renovation of an 80m² apartment in Bilbao comes in significantly below market rates (which should be around €40,000-80,000 for a full reform), this is a red flag. Either materials will be poor quality, experienced workers will be replaced with apprentices, or costs that should be included will appear later as "extras."
  4. Not checking company credentials — In Bizkaia, any company doing electrical or plumbing work needs to be registered with the relevant guild ( Gremio de Electricistas, Gremio de Fontaneros). Ask for their guild membership and verify it. Also check if they have liability insurance. A reform that floods your neighbor's apartment because of a botched pipe job will cost you far more than a few extra euros on a reputable company.
  5. Starting work without a written contract — Verbal agreements are worth nothing in a renovation dispute. Your contract should include: detailed scope of work, materials specification (brand, model, quality), payment schedule tied to milestones, timeline with specific dates, penalties for delays, and who is responsible for permits.
  6. Not researching licenses in Bilbao — For most interior renovations in Bilbao, you need at least a "comunicación previa" (prior notification). For work involving structural changes, changing the use of a room, or work in buildings in the Casco Viejo (which has special protection as a BIC), you need a full "licencia de obra mayor." Doing work without the right license can result in fines of €1,000-10,000.
  7. Changing your mind mid-reform — Every change you make once work has started costs 2-3 times more than if you'd planned for it from the beginning. The renovation equivalent of "while we're at it, let's move this wall" is the fastest way to double your budget and extend your timeline by months. Decide everything before demolition starts.
  8. Buying materials yourself to save money — This only works if you really know the market. Professional reformers get 20-40% discounts from suppliers because they buy in volume and have long-standing relationships. If you buy tiles or sanitary ware yourself, you'll likely pay more and might buy the wrong specifications. The one exception: if you're choosing high-end finishes and want to see them first-hand.
  9. Not visiting the property during work — You don't need to be there every day, but regular check-ins (2-3 times per week) catch problems early. We've seen cases where a "professional" installed pipes backwards, laid flooring without leveling the base, or cut corners on waterproofing. Daily photos from the workers help if you can't be there in person.
  10. Underestimating the emotional toll — Living in a construction zone for 2-3 months is exhausting. If you're living in the property during the reform (common in Bilbao where rent is expensive), plan for: dust containment, alternative kitchen arrangements, possible loss of water/electricity on workdays. The physical and emotional stress of a renovation is real — don't underestimate it.
  11. Not planning for furniture and belongings — Before work starts, you need to: remove all furniture from work areas (or pay the company to do it and insure it), cover immovable items with protective sheets, take photos of everything before work begins. We know of one case where a worker accidentally threw out a client's antique furniture because it looked like construction debris.
  12. Forgetting about the end date — Make sure your contract specifies a completion date and includes penalties for delays. In Bilbao, some companies will happily start your job and then disappear for weeks to handle "more urgent" work. If there's a penalty for every week of delay, they're more motivated to finish on time.

Real Cost of These Mistakes in Bilbao

Let's look at what these mistakes actually cost in real terms for a Bilbao renovation:

  • Only getting one budget: Could pay €8,000-15,000 more than necessary on an €50,000 reform
  • No contingency fund: Discovering lead pipes that need replacing mid-reform can add €3,000-8,000 to the bill
  • Choosing cheapest without checking: If they do a bad job on waterproofing, fixing the resulting damp and mold issues costs €2,000-5,000
  • No written contract: If there's a dispute, you have no legal leg to stand on
  • Doing work without license: Fines from €1,000 up, and potential issues when selling the property
  • Mid-reform changes: Each "while we're at it" change typically adds 2-3 weeks and €2,000-4,000

The Total Potential Cost of These Mistakes

On a typical 80m² apartment renovation in Bilbao (€50,000 budget), these common mistakes could add €15,000 to €30,000 in unexpected costs. Taking a few days to plan properly before starting is the best investment you'll ever make.

How to Avoid All These Mistakes

The good news: all of these mistakes are 100% avoidable with proper planning. Here's your checklist:

Pre-Reform Checklist (Do This Before Signing Anything)

  • Request at least 3 detailed, line-by-line budgets
  • Add 15-25% contingency to your budget (higher for older buildings)
  • Verify company credentials: guild membership, insurance, years in business
  • Get everything in writing: contract, specifications, timeline, penalties
  • Research what licenses you need in Bilbao/Bizkaia
  • Finalize all design decisions before work starts
  • Establish a communication plan with the company (who to contact, how often)
  • Document the property thoroughly with photos before work begins
  • Plan where you'll live during the reform
  • Know your payment schedule — never pay 100% upfront
